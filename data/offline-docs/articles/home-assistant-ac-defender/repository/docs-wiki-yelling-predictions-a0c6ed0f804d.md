# Yelling Predictions

Forecast desk · more than forty possible scenarios · zero fake microphones

This is the complete story ledger behind the Energy page. The app selects a band using the
projected month-end bill. Everything after that selection is a **playful possible scenario**, not
a measured decibel value, diagnosis, probability, or promise that any person will react that way.

## What the app actually knows

| Known input | Source | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Projected month-end dollars | Electricity budget snapshot | A projection changes with the date, rate configuration, and input freshness |
| Month-to-date electricity spend | Real Alectra samples or the documented AC-only estimate | The estimate is not a whole-house meter |
| Cooling runtime | Real Home Assistant `hvac_action = cooling` history | Runtime does not reveal why a person changed the thermostat |
| Thermostat changes | Real audit and context metadata | A device change is not speech, pain, anger, or danger evidence |
| Similarity to labelled upset moments | Locally trained interaction model | It is a thermostat-interaction risk cue, not a prediction that somebody will yell |

The app has **no microphone, calibrated sound meter, camera emotion model, or personal-danger
classifier**. Numeric dB claims and invented percentages were removed because the app had no
instrument or dataset that could support them.

## All ten forecast bands

### $0–$59.99 · SERENE

Possible sequence:

1. The envelope or PDF is opened without an emergency audience.
2. The thermostat avoids becoming Exhibit A.
3. One approving nod may occur.
4. No follow-up lecture is forecast.
5. The month becomes the quiet comparison baseline.

### $60–$99.99 · EYEBROW

Possible sequence:

1. The total gets a second look before filing.
2. Somebody asks whether the AC ran during on-peak hours.
3. The Energy page is opened to answer the question.
4. The current month is compared with the previous one.
5. The discussion ends after one raised eyebrow instead of a summit.

### $100–$139.99 · HMPH

Possible sequence:

1. The bill is held up like a suspicious cheque.
2. Somebody asks who changed the temperature.
3. Somebody asks whether a window or door was open.
4. The calendar is searched for one unusually expensive day.
5. A rhetorical walk-in-fridge question returns at dinner.

### $140–$179.99 · LECTURE WEATHER WATCH

Possible sequence:

1. The month-to-date graph becomes a prop.
2. On-peak hours are read aloud with extra punctuation.
3. A proposal to raise the target by 1 °C appears.
4. Yesterday's wall touches are compared with today's memory of them.
5. A calm factual review can still prevent the full-yell band.

### $180–$209.99 · FULL YELL

Possible sequence:

1. The bill is placed on the dining table as primary evidence.
2. Full names and line items enter the conversation.
3. Every vent receives an unsolicited performance review.
4. The remembered one-fan summer story enters the record.
5. A bounded thermostat peace gesture is proposed.
6. A shorter dinner aftershock follows when the AC cycles on.

### $210–$249.99 · ENCORE YELL

Possible sequence:

1. The same kWh number is quoted twice with different emphasis.
2. The phone app and wall thermostat are accused of conspiring.
3. A tour of closed doors and open vents begins.
4. The budget guard's saved dollars become the strongest defence.
5. A temporary +1 °C truce is requested.
6. The first explanation returns as an encore.

### $250–$274.99 · OPERA

Possible sequence:

1. Movement I audits the current invoice.
2. Movement II compares this summer with every remembered summer.
3. Movement III proposes new thermostat custody rules.
4. The breaker becomes a topic but never an automatic instruction.
5. A quiet pause becomes safer and more useful than another graph.

### $275–$299.99 · BREAKER SUMMIT

Possible sequence:

1. One person volunteers to become the sole thermostat administrator.
2. The vendor schedule is blamed even if its authorship is not proven.
3. A physical thermostat inspection becomes likely.
4. Tamper Truce may observe a real disappearance but will not invent one.
5. The discussion moves to a calm time—or a person leaves if it feels unsafe.

### $300–$349.99 · ULTRA OMEGA YELL

Possible sequence:

1. Prior arguments return as a greatest-hits compilation.
2. Snacks are jokingly reclassified as shared budget assets.
3. `BROTHER MAD` is considered for its real two-hour thermostat stand-down.
4. A manual thermostat change remains a real command with real consequences.
5. Quiet distance, a charged phone, and an exit path matter more than winning.

### $350+ · BEYOND THE METER

The number is outside the storytelling range. Replace prediction with verification:

1. Check whether the projection is based on fresh Alectra data or a fallback.
2. Verify dates, TOU rates, delivery charges, tax, rebate, and duplicate samples.
3. Move the discussion to a calm time.
4. Treat threats, violence, hearing pain, or medical symptoms as real safety matters.
5. Record what actually happened; never promote a forecast into a fact.

## What may happen next by phase

| Phase | Possible, not guaranteed | Useful response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the invoice | Questions about rate periods, open doors, setpoints, or who changed what | Verify inputs and show timestamps |
| First minute | A raised voice, repeated total, or demand for an immediate setting change | Lower your own voice and use one fact at a time |
| Equipment phase | Vent tour, thermostat inspection, phone-app audit, or breaker talk | Keep electrical work deliberate and never act from comedy copy |
| Memory phase | Prior summers and old bills replace the current measurement | Schedule a later like-for-like comparison |
| Truce phase | A bounded setpoint change or `BROTHER MAD` stand-down is proposed | State exactly what the real command will do |
| Aftershock phase | The topic returns when the compressor starts or at the next meal | Use the saved factual record, then pause again |
| Unsafe phase | Threats, violence, blocked exits, fear, hearing pain, or medical symptoms | Leave if possible and call 9-1-1 for immediate danger or a medical emergency |

## My freeze-and-cry response: what I may experience next

This is the lived household fact the timeline starts from: **when my brother yells at me, I can get
a fight-or-flight response, my body can freeze, and I can cry.** The order below is possible, not
guaranteed. A stage can be skipped, repeated, or felt differently. It is not a diagnosis and AC
Defender cannot detect it.

> **At-any-time safety override:** threats, violence, a blocked exit, or fear for safety are not a
> later stage in a timeline. Leave if possible and call **9-1-1** for immediate danger.

| Possible stage | What I may notice | What should happen for survival |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The yelling starts | My body may react before I can plan; muscles may tighten and attention may lock onto the voice | Stop the bill discussion and orient toward safety |
| Fight-or-flight alarm | My heart or breathing may feel faster, or I may feel hot, shaky, dizzy, sick, or intensely alert | Create safe distance and reduce noise; nobody should argue about whether the response is reasonable |
| Freeze | I may become very still, feel unable to move, or struggle to make words come out | Do not demand an answer; allow time and an unblocked exit |
| Crying | I may cry even while trying to speak or leave | Treat tears as a reason to pause, not as agreement, guilt, or manipulation |
| Narrow attention | Several questions or choices may become hard to process | Use one calm sentence and make no bill, thermostat, money, or account decision |
| Exit attempt | If movement returns, I may need a quieter place or a trusted person | Do not follow, grab, corner, or block the route |
| Grounding | The floor, visible objects, or a familiar object may help me reconnect with the present | Offer a technique only if it is safe; never force it, and stop if it feels worse |
| First quiet minutes | I may keep crying, shaking, feeling numb, or needing silence after yelling stops | Allow recovery time; quiet, water, or a familiar safe place may help if comfortable |
| Thinking returns | Words and planning may return gradually rather than all at once | Ask whether I want company, quiet, or a trusted-person call; accept the answer |
| Replay and what-if loop | My brain may replay words, search for what I missed, predict another argument, or try to design the perfect response | Write the worries once, separate known facts from feared possibilities, and choose one safe next action |
| Sleep and concentration after-effects | The loop may make it harder to focus, decide, relax, or sleep, and tiredness may make tomorrow's worrying louder | Defer major decisions, protect a quiet sleep routine, and seek support if the pattern persists or disrupts daily life |
| Later that day | I may feel tired, upset, embarrassed, watchful, or unwilling to restart the topic | Do not shame the response or force a same-day bill meeting |
| Next conversation | The voice, room, bill, or thermostat topic may bring the alarm back sooner | Use the planned pause signal, calm setting, clear exit, and one fact at a time |
| Repeated or worsening responses | Freeze, crying, watchfulness, or renewed alarm may become frequent, worsen, feel highly distressing, or disrupt sleep, school, work, relationships, or daily life | A healthcare or mental-health professional can discuss non-emergency support without the app assigning a diagnosis |

Government of Canada guidance describes fight/flight/freeze as an automatic stress response rather
than a choice under direct control. It notes that the response can include faster heart rate and
breathing, tightened muscles, and sharper senses, while individual experiences vary. Source:
[Stress and Performance](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/corporate/reports-publications/health/r2mr-aide-memoire/stress-and-performance.html).

The replay and what-if rows are possible explanations, not a scan of one person's brain. Public
guidance supports the surrounding facts: stress and anxiety can include excessive worry, tension,
concentration or decision difficulty, and loss of sleep; thoughts, feelings, and behaviour can form
an unhelpful cycle; and writing worries down, separating solvable from hypothetical worries,
bounded “worry time,” present focus, and evidence-checking are options people can try. Sources:
[NIMH, “I’m So Stressed Out!”](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/so-stressed-out-fact-sheet),
[NHS, Tackling your worries](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/tackling-your-worries/),
and [NHS, Reframing unhelpful thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/reframing-unhelpful-thoughts/).

## Behaviour and configuration

The Yell-O-Meter reads `Budget.ProjectedMonthEndCad` and selects the last configured story band
whose threshold is not greater than that value. `YellThreshold` remains `$180` for the critical
banner. The bands do not alter thermostat logic, the monthly budget, guard precedence, or Home
Assistant state.

There is no user-adjustable claim that `$180` causes a particular human reaction. It is the app's
named storytelling line. Real cost configuration lives in Energy & Costs.

## Failure modes

- A bad or stale input can select the wrong story band.
- The correct band can still predict the wrong human response because it is not a behaviour model.
- A thermostat change can be misattributed when Home Assistant context is absent; the app must
  show uncertainty rather than invent authorship.
- A medical or personal-safety event is outside the Yell-O-Meter's capabilities.

## Security and privacy

Do not add names, recordings, private messages, or conflict transcripts to make the forecast seem
more personal. Search is local. Exports may contain real entity names and timestamps; review them
before sharing.
